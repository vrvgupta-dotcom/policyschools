Speaking Topics

AI Governance

AI Governance's Blind Spot

Why AI governance is taught as a bias and opacity problem when the more consequential risk is architectural standardisation that excludes anyone outside the norm — and what that means for policy design.

02

Regulation & Liability

The EU AI Act, Vendor Liability & the Accommodation Gap

Connecting the EU AI Act's high-risk classification of recruitment tools, the Mobley v. Workday doctrine, and what audit, accommodation, and disclosure mandates would need to look like to close the gap.

Developing States

Designing AI Governance for Developing States

Why states building AI frameworks alongside rapid digital infrastructure rollout are at the highest risk of encoding exclusion at the foundation — and what governance architecture, procurement standards, and regulatory sequencing need to look like to avoid it.

How a single custodial violation becomes a structural constitutional accountability mechanism. A live case study in rights litigation strategy — and what it reveals about institutional design failures in practice.

Current Litigation

Delhi High Court · The Working Example

W.P.(C) 11495/2025

The governance argument in every talk is grounded in a live case: a self-represented constitutional petition arising from custodial disability rights violations by Delhi Police and Tihar Jail. Where most AI governance scholarship works from hypotheticals, this case provides a concrete instance of what institutional design failure looks like when it reaches a real person — and how the absence of systems integration becomes a justiciable harm.
